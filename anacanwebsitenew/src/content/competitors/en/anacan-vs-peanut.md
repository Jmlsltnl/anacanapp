Quick answer: **Peanut** and **Anacan** actually solve different problems. Peanut is a community/matching app that connects women in similar life stages to help them make friends — it isn't built for data tracking. **Anacan** combines full cycle, pregnancy, and baby tracking, a partner mode, and Anacan.AI with its own community feature, all **in one app**. Here's the full comparison.

## What is Peanut?

Peanut was founded in 2016 and is headquartered in London, UK. It positions itself as "the app for women, by women" — a social networking and matching app that connects women going through similar life stages: trying to conceive, pregnancy, motherhood, and even a dedicated "Peanut Menopause" community. In practice, it feels much more like a swipe-to-connect social app for moms than a data-tracking tool.

## Where Anacan and Peanut are similar

- Both include a community feature for connecting with other mothers
- Both are built specifically around the motherhood/parenting journey
- Both offer a free tier, with optional premium features on top

## What makes Anacan different

**Tracking + community, together.** Because Peanut is purpose-built as a social-matching app, it doesn't offer a cycle calendar, ovulation calculator, week-by-week pregnancy tracker, baby growth charts, or a vaccine schedule. Anacan brings its community feature together with all of these tracking tools.

**Partner mode.** Anacan has a dedicated mode so partners/dads can stay involved day to day; Peanut has no equivalent.

**Anacan.AI.** A 24/7 AI health assistant responds based on your exact stage — cycle day, pregnancy week, or baby's age — something Peanut doesn't offer.

**Native content in 5 languages.** Anacan is built natively for Azerbaijani, English, Russian, Turkish, and Kazakh speakers; Peanut is designed primarily for English-speaking markets and isn't localized for this region.

**Local context.** Features like Azerbaijan's national vaccination schedule and a maternity-leave calculator are specific to Anacan.

## Peanut's strengths

To be fair, Peanut does one thing very well: helping women build genuine friendships during a stage of life that can feel isolating. Its "meet moms nearby" format is a dedicated, well-designed way to fight loneliness, especially for new mothers without a support network close by. That's a real strength, not a minor add-on.

## Which should you choose?

**Choose Anacan if:**
- You want to track your whole journey — cycle through motherhood — in one app
- You want partner mode, an AI assistant, and community all together
- You need content that's genuinely localized in Azerbaijani, Russian, Turkish, or Kazakh
- You want practical local tools like a vaccine calendar or maternity-leave calculator

**Peanut might be a better fit if you mainly want to meet other moms nearby:**
- Your main goal is meeting moms nearby or at the same life stage as you
- Community and friend-matching matter more to you than tracking tools
- You're comfortable in English and want a large, established global community

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I use Anacan and Peanut together?** Yes — since they solve different needs, many mothers use Peanut for community and Anacan for tracking and AI guidance side by side.

**Does Peanut have a cycle calendar or other tracking tools?** No. Peanut is built as a social/community-matching app, not a tracker — it doesn't offer cycle, pregnancy, or baby-tracking tools.

**Is Peanut available in Azerbaijani, Turkish, Kazakh, or Russian?** Peanut is designed mainly for English-speaking markets and doesn't offer native localization in these languages; Anacan is built natively in all five.

**Which app is better for fighting loneliness and meeting other moms?** That's Peanut's core strength — its whole design centers on community and friend-matching. Anacan also has a community feature, but it's paired with full tracking tools rather than standing alone.
