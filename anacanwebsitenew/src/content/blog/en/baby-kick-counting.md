Your baby's movements are the simplest indicator of their well-being — which is why doctors recommend daily movement tracking from week 28. The rule is simple: learn your baby's "normal rhythm" and notice deviations from it in time. This guide explains when to start counting, how to count and when to contact a doctor immediately.

## When do you feel the first kicks?

In a first pregnancy, movements are usually felt between **weeks 18–25**; in later pregnancies even earlier — from week 16. At first they feel like "butterfly wings" or "bubbles", and over the weeks they turn into unmistakable kicks.

An anterior placenta (placenta on the front wall) can make movements feel later and fainter — that's normal, but it doesn't replace tracking.

## How to count: the "10 movements" method

1. Pick your baby's most active time (many babies liven up in the evening)
2. Lie down comfortably — lying on your left side improves circulation
3. Log every movement: kicks, rolls, pushes all count (hiccups don't)
4. Track the time it takes to reach **10 movements**

For healthy babies, 10 movements usually accumulate in **under 2 hours** — often within 15–30 minutes. [Anacan's kick counter](/en/features/) measures each session automatically and builds a day-to-day comparison chart.

## What's normal — and what isn't?

Every baby has their own rhythm: some are morning-active, some evening-active. That's why your **baby's own usual pattern** matters more than absolute numbers. Sleep cycles are normal too — babies in the womb sleep in 20–40 minute cycles (sometimes up to 90 minutes).

**Contact your doctor immediately if:**

- Movements have clearly decreased or stopped
- You haven't felt 10 movements within 2 hours
- The pattern has changed sharply (an always-active baby has gone quiet)

Tips like "eat something sweet and wait" or "buzz your phone at the bump" waste precious time — international guidance is unambiguous: seek assessment as soon as you notice a decrease.
