A newborn sleeps 14–17 hours a day — but in 2–4 hour stretches. In the first months this is completely normal: a baby's stomach is tiny and needs frequent feeding. The good news: from around 3–4 months, sleep gradually consolidates and a routine becomes possible. In this guide you'll find sleep norms by age, safe-sleep rules and practical steps to build a routine.

## How much should a baby sleep by age?

| Age | Total sleep (24h) | Daytime naps |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 months | 14–17 hours | 4–6 short naps |
| 4–6 months | 12–15 hours | 3–4 naps |
| 7–9 months | 12–15 hours | 2–3 naps |
| 10–12 months | 11–14 hours | 2 naps |

## Safe sleep rules (SIDS prevention)

International guidance on reducing the risk of sudden infant death syndrome (SIDS) is unambiguous:

- Always put the baby to sleep **on their back** — for nights and naps
- Keep the crib **empty**: no pillows, blankets, toys or bumpers
- Baby should sleep **in the parents' room but in their own bed** (first 6 months)
- Keep the room at 18–22°C / 64–72°F and don't overdress the baby
- Keep the environment smoke-free

## What is the 4-month sleep regression?

Around 4 months, a baby's sleep architecture matures — sleep cycles shorten and the baby wakes more often between cycles. It's a healthy developmental sign, but an exhausting stretch for parents. It typically lasts 2–6 weeks. A steady ritual and identical sleep conditions are your biggest allies here.

## 5 steps to build a routine

1. **Create a consistent bedtime ritual:** bath → massage → feed → lullaby. Same order every night.
2. **Teach day vs night:** bright and lively during the day, dark and quiet at night.
3. **Watch wake windows:** don't miss tiredness cues (eye rubbing, yawning).
4. **Put baby down drowsy but awake:** let them learn to fall asleep in the crib.
5. **Keep records:** routines are built on data — a sleep log reveals the patterns.
