Pregnancy lasts about 40 weeks (280 days) and is divided into three trimesters. Each trimester has its own character: organs form in the first, the baby grows rapidly and movements become noticeable in the second, and the body prepares for birth in the third. This guide explains what happens to your baby and your body at each stage — and which weeks deserve special attention.

## First trimester (weeks 1–13): the foundation

The first trimester is the most critical period — these are the weeks when your baby's heart, brain, spine and all major organs form. A heartbeat is usually visible on ultrasound by week 6. By the end of week 12 the baby is roughly 5–6 cm long, with every organ in place.

For the mother, this stage often brings nausea, fatigue and frequent urination. Folic acid matters most right now — it lowers the risk of neural tube defects.

## Second trimester (weeks 14–27): the golden period

The second trimester is the most comfortable stretch for many mothers: nausea fades and energy returns. The baby grows fast — around week 20 it is banana-sized (about 25 cm), and by week 27 it reaches roughly 36 cm.

The sweetest moment happens here too: the first movements. In a first pregnancy, kicks are usually felt between weeks 18–25. The anatomy scan around week 20 evaluates your baby's development in detail.

## Third trimester (weeks 28–40): preparing for the finish

In the final trimester your baby mainly gains weight while the lungs mature. From week 28, doctors recommend tracking movements daily — a [kick counter](/en/features/) is the easiest way to do it. Between weeks 32–36 the baby usually settles head-down.

After week 37 a pregnancy is considered full term. From here, timing contractions with the 5-1-1 rule (contractions every 5 minutes, lasting 1 minute, for 1 hour) tells you precisely when it's time to head to the hospital.

## When should you call a doctor immediately?

Contact your doctor or emergency care without waiting if you notice:

- Heavy bleeding or sharp abdominal pain
- A clear decrease or stop in your baby's movements
- Severe headache, vision changes, sudden swelling of face and hands (signs of preeclampsia)
- Regular contractions or waters breaking before week 37
- A fever above 38°C / 100.4°F

## Trimesters at a glance

| Stage | Weeks | What happens to baby | What happens to you |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st trimester | 1–13 | Organs form, heart starts beating | Nausea, fatigue |
| 2nd trimester | 14–27 | Rapid growth, first movements | Energy returns, bump shows |
| 3rd trimester | 28–40 | Weight gain, lung maturation | Birth prep, practice contractions |
