Your menstrual cycle is not just "those days of the month" — it's a monthly report on your hormonal health. A normal cycle ranges from 21 to 35 days and moves through four phases. Tracking it helps you understand your energy waves, plan (or make informed contraception decisions) and catch health issues early.

## The 4 phases of the cycle

**1. Menstrual phase (days 1–5).** The uterine lining renews itself. Energy tends to be low — give your body rest and choose iron-rich foods.

**2. Follicular phase (days 1–13).** Estrogen rises and an egg matures. Energy and mood climb step by step — an ideal stretch for starting new things.

**3. Ovulation (~day 14).** The mature egg is released and lives 12–24 hours. Because sperm can survive up to 5 days, the fertile window is the 5 days before ovulation plus ovulation day itself.

**4. Luteal phase (days 15–28).** Progesterone takes over. Toward the end, PMS signs (bloating, irritability, poor sleep) may appear. This phase's length is usually stable: 12–14 days.

## How can you pinpoint ovulation?

- **Calendar method:** count back ~14 days from your next period (the app does this automatically)
- **Body signals:** egg-white-like clear discharge, mild lower-belly ache, higher libido
- **Ovulation tests:** detect the LH surge — the most accurate at-home method

## What does tracking actually reveal?

Logging at least 3 cycles shows you your personal pattern: your real cycle length, which days PMS starts, and in which phase your energy peaks. At the doctor's office these records are gold — you'll have an exact answer to "how long is your cycle?".

**When to see a doctor:** if your cycle is consistently shorter than 21 or longer than 35 days, bleeding lasts more than 7 days, pain disrupts daily life, or you've had no period for over 3 months.
